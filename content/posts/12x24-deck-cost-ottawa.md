A 12x24 deck is long and shallow. It runs across the back of the house, gives every door and window a view of the yard, and leaves more lawn than a square deck of the same area. It is a common shape on Ottawa townhouses and on homes with a walkout where the deck sits a full storey up. So it makes sense that **12x24 deck cost** is a question so many people ask.

The short answer: a 12x24 deck in Ottawa typically costs about **$8,640 to $12,960 in pressure-treated wood** and about **$18,720 to $28,800+ in composite**, installed and before tax. Railings and stairs are extra, and on a raised 12x24 deck they make up a big part of the total.

## 12x24 deck cost in Ottawa by material

A 12x24 deck is 288 square feet. At our typical installed prices in Ottawa, before tax, the deck itself costs:

- **Pressure-treated** ($30 to $45 per sq ft): about **$8,640 to $12,960**
- **Cedar** ($50 to $70 per sq ft): about **$14,400 to $20,160**
- **Composite** ($65 to $100+ per sq ft): about **$18,720 to $28,800+**
- **PVC** ($85 to $120+ per sq ft): about **$24,480 to $34,560+**

That covers footings, framing, decking and labour. Railings, stairs and extras are priced separately.

## Railing for a 12x24 deck

The long, shallow shape is efficient for railing when the 24 ft side runs along the house.

### Long side against the house

Open sides: 12 + 24 + 12 = 48 linear feet. With a 4 ft stair opening, you need about **44 linear feet** of railing.

- **Wood** ($60 to $80 per linear ft): about $2,640 to $3,520
- **Aluminum** ($90 to $140 per linear ft): about $3,960 to $6,160
- **Glass** ($180 to $250 per linear ft): about $7,920 to $11,000

### Short side against the house

Turn the deck so it sticks out 24 ft into the yard and the open sides become 24 + 12 + 24 = 60 linear feet, or about **56 linear feet** with a stair opening. Aluminum would then run about $5,040 to $7,840 and glass about $10,080 to $14,000. That is why most 12x24 decks run along the house.

### Is a railing required?

Ontario's Building Code generally requires a guard (railing) when a deck's walking surface is more than 600 mm (24 in.) above the ground. A second-storey 12x24 deck always needs one. A ground-level deck may not. Confirm your project with the City of Ottawa. See railing styles on our [railings page](/railings).

## Ground-level vs second-storey 12x24 deck

Height changes the cost more than anything else on a deck this shape.

### Ground-level

A low 12x24 deck off a patio door needs short posts, simple framing and a few steps. It may not need a railing or a permit. This is the most affordable way to build this size.

### Second-storey

A 12x24 deck off a kitchen on a walkout lot sits a full storey up. It needs tall posts, larger beams, bracing, a full railing, a full flight of stairs and a building permit. In Ottawa, an attached deck more than 600 mm (24 in.) above the ground needs a permit at any size. Our [deck permit guide](/post/deck-permit-ottawa) explains the rules.

The space under a second-storey deck is a bonus. It can stay open as a shaded patio, or be closed in with skirting for storage.

## Making a 12x24 deck work well

A long, shallow deck has its own design rules.

- **Plan the zones in a line.** BBQ near the kitchen door, dining in the middle, lounge at the far end.
- **Think about stair placement.** Stairs at one end keep the deck open. Stairs in the middle split it in two.
- **Watch the depth.** At 12 ft deep, a dining table fits with room to walk around it. Go much shallower and furniture gets tight.
- **Consider privacy.** On townhouses, a privacy screen on one end blocks the neighbour's view. It is priced on its own.
