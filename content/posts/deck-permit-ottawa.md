It is the first question on a lot of our consultations: **do I need a deck permit in Ottawa?** The short answer is that it depends on two things. How high the deck sits above the ground, and whether it is attached to your house.

This guide walks through the City of Ottawa's rules in plain English. What triggers a permit, what zoning still applies when you do not need one, what the inspections look like and how to apply. Rules can change, so always confirm your own project with the City before you build.

## When you need a deck permit in Ottawa

The City of Ottawa sets out three cases where a deck needs a building permit.

- **Attached to or beside the house:** a permit is required if the walking surface is more than 600 mm (24 in.) above the ground next to it. Size does not matter.
- **Elevated deck that is the main way into the house:** a permit is required no matter the size.
- **Freestanding deck** (for example, in the middle of the yard): a permit is required if the walking surface is more than 600 mm (24 in.) above the ground **and** the deck is larger than 10 square metres (about 108 sq ft).

> The magic number is 600 mm (24 in.). An attached deck above that height needs a permit at any size. A low, ground-level deck usually does not, but zoning rules still apply.

### What "600 mm above grade" means

The height is measured from the walking surface of the deck down to the ground beside it. On a sloped yard, one corner of the deck can be low while the far side is well above 24 inches. If any part of the deck crosses that line, plan on a permit.

### Common Ottawa examples

- A ground-level composite deck off the patio door, a step or two up from the lawn: usually no permit, but check zoning.
- A raised deck off the kitchen on a walkout lot: permit required.
- A second-storey deck with stairs to the yard: permit required.
- A floating platform at the back of the yard, low to the ground: usually no permit.

## Zoning rules apply even without a permit

This is the part many homeowners miss. The City is clear that you must check zoning **whether or not** your deck needs a building permit. The main concern is how close the deck sits to your lot lines.

Setbacks depend on your zone and lot, so there is no single number for all of Ottawa. The City's Development Information Officers can tell you what applies to your property. You can reach the City through 3-1-1 or a Client Service Centre.

If you are planning a deck close to a fence or side lot line, sort this out before you design. It is much cheaper to change a drawing than to move a deck.

## How to apply for a deck permit in Ottawa

The City takes building permit applications online through its permitting portal, which you reach through a My ServiceOttawa account. In-person service at Client Service Centres is by appointment.

A few things to know:

- **Payment goes in with the application.** The fee is set by the City's building by-law. Check the City's current fee schedule for the amount.
- **The first review takes about 5 business days**, according to the City. Incomplete applications take longer.
- **Plans and details.** The City reviews your deck for Ontario Building Code compliance and zoning. Expect to show the size, height, footings, framing, stairs and guards, plus where the deck sits on your lot. The City has a handout on what a complete package looks like.
- **Post the permit on site** for the whole build, and keep the approved plans there too.
- **Start on time.** The City can revoke a permit if construction does not seriously start within six months of issue.

We design every deck in 3D before we build, so the layout, height, stairs and railings are worked out early. Ask us at your estimate how the permit fits into your project timeline.

## Inspections during the build

A permitted deck gets inspected at set stages. Per the City, the stages are:

1. **Footings and foundations:** piers or pads are checked before backfill.
2. **Framing:** once the structural framing and guards are done.
3. **Final:** when the framing, stairs and guards are complete.

Inspections are booked 48 hours ahead, online or through the inspector named on the permit. Each one has to pass before the next stage is covered up. That is why a good builder plans the schedule around them.

## Railings, stairs and hot tubs

### Railings (guards)

The Ontario Building Code sets the rules for guards on decks, including when they are needed and how high they must be. In general, a deck more than 600 mm above the ground needs a guard. The City checks guards at both the framing and final inspections. See our [aluminum and glass railings](/railings), which we install to code.

### Stairs

Stairs are part of the permitted work and are checked at the final inspection. Rise, run, width and handrails all matter, so they belong in the drawings from day one.

### Hot tubs

A hot tub on a deck adds weight, so the structure has to be designed for it. Separately, the City treats a hot tub that can hold 600 mm (24 in.) of water or more under its Pool Enclosure By-law, which can require a pool enclosure permit even if the tub has a locking lid. Ask the City about your specific tub before you buy.

## What happens if you build without a permit?

Skipping a required permit means no one inspects the footings, framing or guards before they are covered up. Unpermitted work can also raise questions when you sell or renovate later. A permit costs far less than rebuilding a deck that was framed wrong.

If you are not sure whether your deck needs one, ask the City or ask us at your estimate. It takes a few minutes to check.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need a permit for a ground-level deck in Ottawa?
Usually not, if the walking surface is 600 mm (24 in.) or less above the ground. You still need to follow zoning rules, especially setbacks from your lot lines.

### How high can a deck be without a permit in Ottawa?
An attached deck can be up to 600 mm (24 in.) above the ground next to it without a permit. Above that, the City requires a building permit at any size.

### Do I need a permit to replace my old deck?
If the new deck is attached and more than 600 mm above grade, the same permit rules apply as for a new deck. Confirm with the City before you start.

### How long does a deck permit take in Ottawa?
The City says the first review takes about 5 business days. Complete drawings help avoid delays.

### Can I see my deck design before applying for a permit?
Yes. We design your deck in 3D first, so you can see the layout, height, stairs and railings and make changes before anything is submitted or built.

### Do I need a permit to put a hot tub on my deck?
The deck itself follows the normal permit rules. A hot tub that can hold 600 mm of water or more may also need a pool enclosure permit under the City's by-law, so check with the City.
