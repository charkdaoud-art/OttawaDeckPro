Snow is on the way, and a lot of homeowners ask us the same thing this time of year: **can you build a deck in winter in Ottawa?** The short answer is that winter deck building in Ottawa is possible, but the cold changes how the work is done. Frozen ground, short days and building code rules all play a part.

This guide explains what really happens when you build in the cold. How footings and frozen soil work, what the City checks, how composite boards behave below freezing, and why winter is often the best season to plan and design. Rules can change, so always confirm your own project with the City.

## Winter deck building in Ottawa: the short answer

A deck is mostly framing, fasteners and boards. None of that stops working at minus ten. The hard part is the ground.

Every deck sits on some kind of foundation. On a raised deck that usually means footings dug into the soil. In an Ottawa winter, that soil freezes, and frozen soil is harder to dig and must not be built on as if it were firm ground. Once the foundation is done right, the rest of the deck can go up in the cold with the right care.

So the real question is not "can it be done?" It is "can the foundation be done properly for this deck, on this site, at this time of year?"

## Footings, frost and frozen ground

Ottawa soil freezes and thaws every year. Water in the soil expands as it freezes and can push footings up. This is called frost heave, and it is the main reason decks shift, crack or pull away from a house.

### What the Ontario Building Code says

The Ontario Building Code sets minimum foundation depths based on the type of soil. For clay or soils that are not clearly defined, the table calls for footings at least 1.2 m deep, and for unheated foundations like decks, not less than the depth of frost penetration. Rock and some well-drained coarse soils have different rules. Your designer or the City can confirm what applies to your lot.

The code also has an exemption for some low decks. A deck can skip the minimum foundation depth if it is:

- not more than one storey,
- not more than 55 m² in area,
- with floor joists not more than 600 mm above the ground,
- not supporting a roof, and
- not attached to another structure, unless it can be shown that movement will not harm that structure.

These are often called floating decks. They sit on the surface on blocks or pads, so frozen ground does not stop the build in the same way.

### The City's cold weather advisory

The City of Ottawa's Building Code Services has a frost check advisory for foundation excavations. It says that during cold weather, the builder must protect the soil from frost penetration during construction. Based on Environment Canada data, the advisory notes that daytime temperatures in Ottawa usually stay below freezing starting in the first week of December.

If an inspector finds that the excavation was not properly protected from frost, the advisory says a frost check report from a geotechnical consultant is required within 48 hours of the foundation inspection. That adds time and cost. It is one more reason to plan the foundation stage carefully if you build in winter.

> Winter deck building in Ottawa comes down to the foundation. Get the footings in below frost and protected from freezing, and the rest of the deck can be built in the cold.

## Permits and inspections in winter

The permit rules do not change with the seasons. The City of Ottawa requires a building permit for a deck that is attached or next to the house with a walking surface more than 600 mm (24 in.) above grade. A freestanding deck needs one when it is over 10 square metres and more than 600 mm above grade. Read our full guide: [Do I need a permit to build a deck in Ottawa?](/post/deck-permit-ottawa)

A permitted deck has three City inspections:

1. **Foundations,** before backfill, to confirm the footings match the code and your plans.
2. **Framing,** when the structure and guards are complete.
3. **Final,** when the framing, stairs and guards are done.

Inspections are booked 48 hours in advance. In winter, the foundation inspection is the one to plan around, because the footings need to stay protected from frost until they are inspected and covered.

## How composite decking handles the cold

Composite and PVC boards are made for Ottawa winters once they are installed. The cold mostly matters on the day they go down.

Composite boards expand and contract with temperature, more along their length than their width. Manufacturers adjust the gaps for cold weather. For example, Trex recommends:

- **Side gaps:** 3/16 in. between boards, in all weather.
- **End-to-end gaps:** 1/8 in. on days warmer than 40°F (about 4°C), and 3/16 in. on colder days.
- **Against the house or a solid object:** 1/4 in. on warmer days, and 1/2 in. below 40°F.

Every brand has its own guide, and we follow the installation guide for the exact product on your deck. Get the gaps wrong in the cold and boards can buckle in July.

### What about wood decks?

Pressure-treated and cedar decks can be framed and boarded in the cold. Staining is another story. Stains and sealers list a minimum application temperature on the label, so a new wood deck is usually stained once warm, dry weather returns in spring. See our [deck staining](/staining) service.

## Pros and cons of winter deck building

### The upside

- **You are ready for spring.** Plan now and your deck can be ready for the first warm weekend, not the middle of summer.
- **Time to decide.** Without the summer rush, you can take your time on layout, materials and railings.

### The downside

- **Foundation work is harder.** Digging frozen ground and protecting footings from frost takes extra effort and care.
- **Short days.** Less daylight means fewer working hours each day.
- **Snow and ice.** Snow has to be cleared from the work area, and storms can pause the job.
- **Finishing waits.** Staining, sodding and planting around the deck usually wait for spring.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can you build a deck in winter in Ottawa?
Yes, with care. The foundation is the hard part, because footings must go below frost depth and be protected from freezing. Once the foundation is done properly, framing and decking can be installed in the cold.

### Can you pour or dig deck footings in frozen ground?
Frozen ground is harder to dig, and the soil under footings has to be kept from freezing. The City's frost check advisory says builders must protect foundation excavations from frost during cold weather.

### Do I still need a permit for a deck built in winter?
Yes. The permit rules are the same all year. A deck attached to or next to your house with a walking surface more than 600 mm above grade needs a permit, no matter the season.

### Can composite decking be installed in cold weather?
Yes. Composite boards expand and contract with temperature, so the gaps change in the cold. Follow the manufacturer's guide. Trex, for example, recommends larger end gaps on days below 40°F (about 4°C).

### Can I stain a new wood deck in winter?
Usually not. Stains and sealers list a minimum application temperature on the label, so most new wood decks are stained in spring once the weather is warm and dry.

### What is the best thing to do in winter if I want a deck?
Plan it. Get your 3D design, choose your materials, prepare the permit and set your budget, so your deck is ready to build when the ground thaws.
